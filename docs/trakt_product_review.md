# Trakt — Commercial Product Review

*A product-level assessment of what Trakt is today, based on capabilities that materially exist in the repository.*

---

## 1. What is Trakt?

**Trakt is a governed portfolio operating system for lenders and asset owners whose loan books have to serve three masters at once: management, investors and regulators — from a single, provable version of the portfolio.**

The problem it solves is not "reporting". It is that a lender running direct originations plus acquired back books, in one or more SPVs, currently maintains several irreconcilable versions of the same book: an ops spreadsheet, a board pack, an investor cashflow model, and an ESMA submission — each rebuilt by hand, each derived differently, none traceable back to the source tape. Trakt collapses that into one canonical portfolio truth, produced once under deterministic controls, then re-used by MI, risk, forecasting, investor reporting, regulatory delivery and an AI query interface.

**Primary users.** Specialist lenders and originators (the repository's reference asset class is UK equity release / lifetime mortgages, with bridge and asset/equipment finance also exercised through the same pathway), together with the operating functions around them: portfolio and finance teams producing management MI; risk teams monitoring concentration limits; operations teams onboarding and running periodic tapes; and the securitisation perimeter — warehouse lenders, trustees, investors and buy-side diligence teams — who consume the outputs.

**Where it sits in the stack.** Below BI and above the loan servicing / origination systems. Trakt is not a system of record for loans; it is the layer that takes whatever the servicer or seller produced, canonicalises it, proves it, and becomes the system of record for *portfolio truth* — the thing that MI, the risk limit tests, the investor pack and the regulatory submission are all derived from.

**Category.** The repository does not support "point solution" or "reporting platform". It supports **portfolio operating system / data-and-control layer**, with a genuine agentic interaction layer on top. The decisive evidence is that a single canonical dataset, one portfolio registry and one calculation engine feed the dashboard, the chat agent, the PowerPoint pack, the Teams card, the Copilot action and the ESMA XML — rather than each capability owning its own extract.

## 2. Commercial product capabilities that exist today

**Portfolio ingestion and canonicalisation.** A gated pipeline takes a raw lender tape in arbitrary format and produces a typed canonical dataset: deterministic column alignment across six matching tiers, type and format standardisation, geographic enrichment (NUTS/ITL), derived fields such as LTV, schema validation, cross-field business-rule validation, and field- and value-level lineage. A canonical field registry of ~460 fields carries per-field regulatory codes for ESMA Annexes 2/3/4/8/9 and consumer profiles (securitisation, BoE, buy-side diligence, internal MI). *Who values it:* operations and data teams. *Why it matters:* this is the step that today consumes analyst weeks per new seller, per new tape format — and it is the step every downstream number depends on.

**Onboarding as a governed workflow, not a data-engineering project.** New clients, migrations and amendments run as case-based workflows with validation, inference, information requests to the client, submission, approval and a separate activation step — no active configuration exists until a human activates it. A product-profile registry lets an asset's structural characteristics (an equity-release loan has no contractual maturity) relax *base MI* requirements without ever weakening regulatory requirements, and capability readiness is emitted per capability (base MI, pipeline MI, risk monitoring, SPV segmentation, regulatory reporting). *This materially replaces analyst work*: onboarding a new book is an operator workflow rather than a bespoke integration.

**Multi-book / multi-SPV portfolio management.** Every loan carries mandatory source-portfolio provenance (direct vs acquired, cohort, seller, acquisition date), enforced fail-closed at onboarding and at the validation gate. An assembler consolidates the latest accepted snapshot per portfolio into one central canonical with a manifest recording included and *excluded* portfolios, row counts, balances, input hashes and a content hash. A portfolio registry then resolves "total", "direct", "acquired" and named cohorts dynamically from metadata — adding a new book changes every answer with no code change. *Who values it:* CFOs, IB counterparties, rating agencies and legal counsel who must split organic origination from acquired back books.

**Point-in-time and time-series MI.** Funded snapshot MI (balances, LTV, rate, borrower age, stratifications, geography), evolution across governed monthly runs, cohort and vintage progression, and period-change/movement analysis with regional and portfolio attribution — "what moved, where, and how much of it was new completions".

**Pipeline MI and forecasting.** Weekly pipeline snapshots with stage funnel, completed-vs-funded reconciliation as an explicit control, and a forward-exposure layer combining funded exposure with expected funding using stage probabilities read from configuration. Notably, no probability is ever invented: a missing probability becomes a disclosed issue. Forecast extrapolation and a funded→forecast bridge are exposed as first-class outputs.

**Concentration and risk-limit monitoring.** A governed concentration test library declares each test's deterministic evaluator, required canonical fields and implementation status; client thresholds are separately versioned and operator-approved. Facility limits are extracted from the client's Schedule 8 and evaluated against actual book concentrations, producing actual/limit/headroom/status plus movement since the prior run. Missing data yields `unavailable` or `needs_review` — never a fabricated pass. *This is a control function, not a chart*: it is the difference between a covenant breach found by Trakt and one found by the warehouse lender.

**Regulatory and investor reporting.** ESMA Annex 12 investor XML (deal-level) and Annex 2 exposure-level regulatory output, projected from canonical and validated against the official XSD. A delivery agent governs the last mile: tenant binding, deterministic run identity, restart safety, an explicit operator approval before publication, and disclosure of everything the builder defaulted, coerced or omitted to reach schema validity. Provenance is deliberately kept *out* of the regulatory template and emitted as a linked companion file, so submissions stay template-clean while remaining traceable. Annex 3 and 9 are catalogued in the registry but deliberately unregistered for delivery — the product is honest about its perimeter.

**Automated management reporting (PPTX).** Investor/board packs are rendered from the *same* API payloads that drive the dashboard, with numbers taken verbatim rather than recomputed, and deck production is itself a governed capability with authorisation, single-flight de-duplication and publication gates (a blocked deck is a distinct, diagnosable outcome, not a silent failure). *This is genuine replacement of reporting-analyst work*, not convenience automation — the pack cannot disagree with the dashboard.

**Governed natural-language MI (MI Agent).** A chat interface over the whole book, described in §4.

**Operations Control Centre.** An operator-facing console covering workflows, batches, deliveries, review queues, scoped and versioned operational rules, audit history, and administrative configuration with draft → validate → activate → rollback plus version comparison and impact analysis. Approvals become versioned rules; nothing publishes automatically.

**Microsoft 365 surface.** M365 Copilot actions call the identical in-process governed capability the React app calls (no HTTP loopback, no second analytics path), with Entra authentication, an artifact registry serving short-lived HMAC-signed download links, and a hard refusal to answer from demo data. A Teams bot handles registration and proactive card delivery, with a deliberate separation: installing the app captures a conversation reference but grants no portfolio access until an operator maps it.

**Governance, controls and auditability** run across all of the above: a single result envelope carrying capability, tenant, portfolio, snapshot, policy, provenance and audit metadata; one structured audit line per governed execution that deliberately excludes answer bodies, loan rows, tokens, paths and signed URLs; run manifests, content hashes and lineage artefacts at every pipeline stage; and a resumable orchestration run-state that halts on a failed readiness gate rather than producing a non-compliant output.

## 3. Product complexity and value proposition

Trakt's difficulty is not volume of code. It is the number of hard, *simultaneously satisfied* constraints:

- **A canonical model that is also a regulatory model.** The field registry is not a data dictionary; each field carries its ESMA code and mandatory/optional priority per annex, so the same canonical row can serve internal MI and a regulatory submission without a second mapping exercise. Building this once is a specialist exercise; keeping MI and regulatory definitions from drifting apart is the part institutions consistently fail at.
- **Fail-closed determinism.** Provenance is mandatory. Missing forecast probabilities are disclosed. Unsupported concentration tests raise a structured implementation request rather than an improvised calculation. A portfolio missing mandatory regulatory fields halts at the gate instead of quietly producing a non-compliant tape. Every one of these is a decision to be *less* impressive and more trustworthy — which is precisely what an institutional buyer is paying for.
- **Lineage that survives the whole journey.** Source portfolio → canonical → central canonical (with an assembler manifest naming what was excluded and why) → MI answer → regulatory row → companion provenance file. That chain is what makes a number defensible to an auditor, a trustee or a rating agency.
- **Configuration as a governed asset.** Client configuration, risk thresholds and operational rules are versioned, compared, impact-assessed, approved and activated — with rollback. This is institutional change control, not a settings page.
- **One truth, many consumers.** The dashboard, chat, deck, Teams card, Copilot response and regulatory output are structurally prevented from disagreeing because they consume the same governed capability and the same payloads. Reconciling four versions of the same number is the recurring cost this removes.

**Why pay for Trakt rather than Excel + PowerPoint + analysts + BI + generic AI?** Because the alternative stack cannot produce a *defensible* number. Generic BI will chart whatever it is given but has no canonical model, no regulatory mapping, no lineage, no fail-closed behaviour and no approval gates. Generic AI tools will answer questions about an uploaded file, but cannot reason across a multi-SPV book, cannot be prevented from inventing a number, and leave no audit trail. Excel plus analysts can do all of it once — but not reproducibly, not at portfolio scale, not with provenance, and not without the key-person risk that every deal team recognises.

**Where value is strongest:** control and regulatory defensibility first; institutional memory second (mappings, thresholds, approvals and rules are captured as governed configuration rather than living in an analyst's head); then speed and scalability (a new book or a new period is a workflow, not a project); with cost reduction real but a consequence rather than the headline.

## 4. AI-native architecture and design

Trakt is legitimately **AI-native**, and the evidence is architectural rather than rhetorical: **the AI is never allowed to produce a number.**

The separation is explicit and enforced. A natural-language question is interpreted by Claude into a declarative query specification — a structured description naming semantic field keys, dimensions, filters, aggregations and an execution mode. That spec is normalised, validated against the semantic registry, stripped of hallucinated field names, and rejected outright if the model returns code rather than a spec. Only then is it executed by a deterministic calculation engine, whose output must satisfy fail-closed dimension and filter invariants and carries a query trace. A fully deterministic offline parser exists as an alternative path, which is the clearest possible proof that the LLM is an interpreter and not the engine. The same pattern repeats elsewhere: the Tier-7 field-mapping agent proposes mappings that require human confirmation and are then learned as deterministic aliases so future runs need no LLM at all; the OCC onboarding agent exposes a bounded, typed tool surface with no general-purpose "do what the text says" entry point, returns proposals a human must confirm for anything material, derives state from what the controls actually returned, and structurally refuses the one operation that would create active configuration.

Governance sits *below* the AI, not beside it. Identity comes from the platform, never from the request — tenant is deployment configuration and can never be a token claim or a request field. Every governed call performs a scope check, portfolio authorisation and a data-source approval check *before the first read*, and production questions can only be answered from an approved assembled canonical; pointing the system at demo data causes a refusal, not a plausible answer. Results return in a uniform envelope carrying tenant, portfolio, snapshot, policy and provenance, and emit one audit line per execution that carefully excludes the answer body and any credential or path.

**Why this matters commercially:** an institutional user cannot deploy an AI that might be right. The value of this architecture is that a hallucination surfaces as a clarification request or a validation error rather than as a wrong balance in a board pack — and that every answer is attributable to a tenant, a portfolio, a snapshot and an approved data source.

**Reasoning across the book, not across a document.** Because the AI operates over the assembled central canonical and the governed portfolio registry, "compare direct vs acquired", "show me acquired_002 versus the whole book" or "what changed since last month" resolve to portfolio lenses and cohorts across every SPV and book in scope, with the applied lens echoed in the answer and excluded portfolios disclosed with reason codes. That is categorically different from a generic Copilot chatting with an uploaded spreadsheet: the scope is the institution's portfolio, resolved dynamically from provenance metadata, under authorisation, with the same calculation engine the dashboard and the regulatory pipeline use.

## Overall assessment

**What Trakt has become.** It began as a regulatory-reporting pipeline and is now something considerably larger: a portfolio operating system for lenders whose books have to satisfy management, investors and regulators simultaneously. The product's centre of gravity is the canonical portfolio — created once, proved once, provenance-stamped, assembled across books and SPVs — and everything else in the product is a governed consumer of it. The breadth is real: ingestion, onboarding, multi-book assembly, funded and pipeline MI, cohort and period-change analysis, forecasting, concentration and covenant monitoring, ESMA delivery, automated packs, operations control, and an AI query layer that spans all of it.

**Strongest differentiators.** First, the canonical-to-regulatory model: internal MI and ESMA output derive from the same field registry, so they cannot drift. Second, cross-capability consistency by construction — the deck, the dashboard, the chat answer and the Teams card are the same numbers because they are literally the same governed payloads. Third, the multi-book/SPV provenance model, which is the part a lender with acquired back books cannot easily build and cannot do without. Fourth, the discipline of disclosed refusal: unavailable, needs_review, halted, blocked and excluded are all first-class outcomes.

**Where defensibility genuinely sits.** Not in any single algorithm. It sits in the accumulated, institutionally-shaped semantics — the field registry with regulatory codes, the alias corpus learned from real tape formats, the concentration test library and its evaluators, the product profiles, the route contracts, the approval and configuration-versioning model — and in the fact that these are wired together with enforced boundaries rather than convention. A competitor can build a dashboard in a quarter. Reproducing a canonical model that satisfies an ESMA XSD, a warehouse covenant schedule and a board pack from one dataset, with lineage, is a multi-year domain exercise.

**Positioning.** The repository does support describing Trakt as **institutional-grade financial infrastructure with an AI-native interaction layer** — with the caveat that the claim should be made in the terms the code actually earns: fail-closed determinism, governed capabilities, enforced identity and source approval, disclosed exclusions, human approval before publication, and an AI confined to interpretation. It should not be positioned as an autonomous agent that manages portfolios, and the repository does not ask it to be.

**Category.** The most accurate description is a **governed portfolio operating system and control layer for private credit and securitised lending — canonical portfolio data infrastructure with an agentic, multi-book interaction layer**. It is not a reporting tool, not a BI product, and not a point solution; the reporting outputs are the visible surface of a control and data layer that is the actual product.
